**H+ctx (danger/strain/arousal context) is rejected.** A calm cat-on-the-windowsill narrative raises the
+ pole as much as the danger context does. What matters is:
1. **Somatosensory/pain discourse flips the whole neuron**: after one sentence about pain/tingling/numbness, every
   + target (strong, injury, nerve) falls to ≈0, and the − pole rises (Δ − +0.16 to +0.40). Strongest effect in the study.
2. **Register**: a business-report context suppresses physical "strong" (danger−business Δ +0.20, fire rate
   0.62 vs 0.12). Calm-narrative vs no context: +0.19 on strong. So "strong" fires when the discourse is
   concrete and physical and is damped in abstract or business text. This explains part of the Q+2 failure,
   because one of the three standard preambles is an office/invoice text.
3. Nerve and injury targets are fairly context-independent (outside the pain frame).
